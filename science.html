The Origin

Your Body Already Makes the World's Best Sanitizer.

Hypochlorous Acid (HOCl) is the molecule your white blood cells (neutrophils) produce to eliminate invading pathogens. When your body detects a threat, neutrophils generate HOCl to destroy bacteria, viruses, and fungi at the cellular level.

VISK brings the same molecule to your farm — produced through electrolysis, not harvested from the body. The result is a powerful antimicrobial solution that is:

  • More effective than bleach at sanitation
  • Completely biodegradable
  • Safe for food contact
  • Gentle on skin, plants, and fish

The Mechanism

How HOCl Eliminates Pathogens

Penetrates the Cell

HOCl's neutral charge lets it pass through the negatively charged cell membrane — something bleach (OCl⁻, negatively charged) cannot do effectively.

Oxidizes from Inside

Once inside, HOCl oxidizes critical cell components — destroying the membrane, the internal body, and the DNA. The pathogen is eliminated within seconds to minutes.

Breaks Down Cleanly

HOCl then reverts to plain water (H₂O) and trace salt (NaCl). No residue remains on produce, equipment, or in water.

Why Resistance Can't Develop

HOCl attacks at the fundamental molecular level — oxidizing proteins and nucleic acids simultaneously across multiple sites. Unlike single-pathway chemicals that mutations can bypass, HOCl's multi-site attack leaves no pathway for resistance evolution.

Source: Envirolyte-Pericom Nigeria — "Micro-organisms do NOT become resistant to Neutral Anolyte"

The Comparison

Same Family. Completely Different.

Both HOCl and bleach (sodium hypochlorite / NaOCl) belong to the chlorine family. But they behave very differently.

Property HOCl (VISK) Bleach (NaOCl)
pH 5.0–6.5 (neutral) 9–13 (highly alkaline)
Sanitizing power 80× more effective Baseline
Residue on produce None — breaks to H₂O Chlorine residue
Safe for food contact Yes (FDA GRAS) Requires heavy rinsing
Effect on taste/colour None (peer-reviewed) Can bleach/damage
Corrosive to equipment No Yes, over time
Microbial resistance None — no resistance Resistance develops
Environmental impact Biodegradable Harmful to aquatic life
Safe around fish Yes, at proper dosage Toxic

The reason: At neutral pH (5.0–6.5), chlorine exists as HOCl — a small, uncharged molecule that slips through microbial cell membranes and eliminates them from inside. At high pH (bleach), chlorine becomes OCl⁻ — a large, negatively charged ion that bounces off cell membranes. Same element, 80× less effective.

Source: Envirolyte-Pericom Nigeria; Paul Fisher, University of Florida

Common Questions

Everything Farmers Ask About VISK & HOCl

Basics

What is VISK?

VISK is a concentrated Hypochlorous Acid (HOCl) solution manufactured by Tristar Pharmaceuticals in Panchkula, Haryana. It's used as a sanitation and hygiene input on farms — for irrigation water treatment, surface and equipment sanitation, post-harvest produce washing, and biosecurity. It eliminates 99.99%+ of bacteria, fungi, and viruses on surfaces and in water — without leaving any chemical residue.

What is HOCl?

HOCl (Hypochlorous Acid) is the molecule your body's white blood cells produce to eliminate invading pathogens. It's made through electrolysis — passing electricity through salt water. It's 80× more effective than bleach, completely biodegradable, and safe for food contact. It's been used in hospitals, food processing, and water treatment for decades.

Is VISK a pesticide?

No. VISK is a sanitation and hygiene input — not a pesticide, fungicide, or plant growth regulator. It does not require CIB&RC registration. It sanitizes your water, surfaces, equipment, and harvested produce. It is not a crop-treatment product.

Safety

Is VISK safe for my crops?

Yes. At recommended dilutions (2–50 ppm depending on use case), VISK has no effect on plant growth, fruit taste, colour, or appearance. This has been confirmed in peer-reviewed studies (Bari et al., 2003 — Journal of Food Protection). VISK breaks down to plain water and trace salt.

Is it safe around fish? Can I use it in aquaponic systems?

Yes, at proper dosages (2–5 ppm). Unlike bleach or copper-based products, HOCl at low concentrations is safe for fish. It's used in aquaculture and aquaponic systems globally for water pathogen control without harming fish or beneficial nitrifying bacteria. Start with lower doses and monitor.

Is it safe for farm workers?

Yes. VISK at working concentrations requires no special PPE beyond normal farm practices. No toxic fumes, no skin irritation, no respiratory hazard. The US FDA classifies HOCl as GRAS (Generally Recognized As Safe) for food contact.

Is it safe for organic farming?

HOCl is compatible with organic farming principles — it's made from salt and water, leaves no synthetic residue, and biodegrades completely. However, certification depends on your specific organic certifying body. We recommend checking with your certifier. VISK can be positioned as a non-chemical hygiene input in organic protocols.

Usage

How do I use VISK?

VISK is a concentrate that you dilute with water based on your use case. The five main applications are: (1) Irrigation water treatment — 2–10 ppm via dosing pump, (2) Surface/equipment sanitation — 200–500 ppm spray, (3) Post-harvest produce wash — 30–50 ppm dip, (4) Greenhouse fogging — 50 ppm mist, (5) Biosecurity foot baths — 200 ppm.

How much VISK do I need per acre?

It depends on your application. For irrigation water treatment at 5 ppm, treating 10,000 litres of water requires just 100 mL of VISK concentrate. A typical greenhouse or polyhouse operation uses 5–20 litres of VISK concentrate per month. Contact us for a custom estimate based on your farm size and water usage.

Do I need special equipment to use VISK?

For irrigation dosing, you'll need a Venturi injector or dosing pump (₹2,000–15,000 depending on capacity). For surface spraying, a standard hand or backpack sprayer works. For produce washing, a food-grade wash tank. For fogging, your existing greenhouse misting system. No specialised equipment is required.

Can I mix VISK with fertilizers or other inputs?

We recommend dosing VISK separately from fertilizers. HOCl is an oxidizer, and mixing it with certain fertilizers (especially organic-based) can reduce its efficacy. Dose VISK upstream of your fertigation injection point, or on a separate cycle.

Comparison

How is VISK different from bleach?

Both are chlorine-based, but they work very differently. VISK (HOCl) is 80× more effective than bleach (NaOCl) at eliminating pathogens because of its neutral pH and uncharged molecular structure. Unlike bleach, VISK leaves no residue, doesn't damage produce, isn't corrosive to equipment, and is safe for fish. See the detailed comparison in the HOCl vs Bleach section above.

How is VISK different from copper-based chemicals?

VISK is a sanitation and hygiene input — it cleans water, surfaces, and produce. Copper-based chemicals are registered crop-protection products. They serve different purposes. Where farmers currently use copper to sanitize surfaces or treat water, VISK offers an alternative that leaves no soil buildup, no residue, and requires no CIB&RC registration. VISK degrades to water and trace salt — copper accumulates in soil over seasons.

Why not just use hydrogen peroxide?

Hydrogen peroxide is a weaker sanitizer — it achieves 3–4 log reduction vs. HOCl's 7+ log reduction. It also breaks down faster in the presence of organic matter (soil, plant debris) and is less stable in storage. HOCl tolerates organic load better and maintains efficacy longer in real farm conditions.
