Common Threats

Why Vineyards Need Year-Round Sanitation

Grapevines face relentless disease pressure from bud break through cold storage — and most vineyard losses trace back to three fungal enemies.

Downy Mildew

Plasmopara viticola

Yellow oily spots on upper leaf surface, white fuzzy growth underneath. Defoliates vines and destroys fruit quality.

VISK Solution: 50 ppm foliar spray every 7 days during the growing season. Increase to every 5 days during monsoon/rainy periods.

Powdery Mildew

Erysiphe necator

White powdery coating on leaves, shoots, and berries. Causes fruit cracking and makes grapes unmarketable.

VISK Solution: 50 ppm foliar spray targeting canopy interior. Preventive sprays from bud break through veraison.

Botrytis Bunch Rot

Botrytis cinerea

Grey mold on grape clusters — especially tight-clustered varieties. Devastates pre-harvest and during storage.

VISK Solution: 50 ppm spray at flower cap-fall and again at veraison. Focus on bunch zone coverage.

Anthracnose (Bird's Eye Spot)

Elsinoe ampelina

Small circular spots with dark margins on berries, leaves, and shoots. Common in young vineyards and humid conditions.

VISK Solution: 50 ppm foliar spray. Begin from bud break in regions with anthracnose history.

Application Protocol

Stage-by-Stage VISK Protocol for Grape

1

Dormant / Pruning

When: Winter

  • Spray pruning tools with 200 ppm between vines. Wash pruning wounds with 100 ppm.
  • Key Risk: Cross-contamination via pruning tools, Eutypa dieback

Dilution: 200 ppm · 40 mL/100L · Tool spray

2

Bud Break

When: Early spring

  • 50 ppm foliar spray as buds open. Begin preventive downy mildew program.
  • Key Risk: Early downy mildew, anthracnose on new shoots

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Foliar spray

3

Shoot Growth

When: Spring

  • 50 ppm spray every 7 days. Focus on new canopy growth. 2–5 ppm via drip for root zone.
  • Key Risk: Powdery mildew establishment, downy mildew spread

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Canopy spray

4

Flowering / Cap-Fall

When: Late spring

  • 50 ppm fine mist at cap-fall — critical Botrytis entry point. Continue downy mildew sprays.
  • Key Risk: Botrytis latent infection through flower remnants

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Fine mist

5

Berry Development

When: Summer

  • 50 ppm foliar spray every 7 days. Ensure bunch-zone penetration.
  • Key Risk: Powdery mildew on berries, downy mildew on leaves

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Directed spray

6

Veraison

When: Late summer

  • 50 ppm spray — second critical Botrytis window as sugar increases. Reduce interval to 5 days if humid.
  • Key Risk: Botrytis bunch rot activation, berry cracking

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Bunch spray

7

Pre-Harvest

When: 1–2 weeks before harvest

  • 30 ppm final spray. Zero residue — safe up to day of harvest.
  • Key Risk: Surface pathogens, fruit quality preservation

Dilution: 30 ppm · 6 mL/100L · Light spray

8

Post-Harvest

When: After picking

  • SO₂ pads are standard, but 30 ppm VISK dip extends cold storage life. Sanitize pack-house surfaces with 200 ppm.
  • Key Risk: Botrytis in cold storage, shelf life reduction

Dilution: 30 ppm dip · 200 ppm surface
