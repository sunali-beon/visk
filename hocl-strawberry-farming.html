Common Threats

Why Strawberries Are So Vulnerable

Strawberries are soft, high-sugar fruit grown close to the ground — making them a magnet for fungal diseases at every stage.

Botrytis (Grey Mold)

Botrytis cinerea

The #1 strawberry disease worldwide. Fuzzy grey growth appears on ripe and near-ripe fruit — can destroy an entire harvest in 48 hours.

VISK Solution: 50 ppm foliar spray every 5–7 days from flowering through harvest.

Anthracnose

Colletotrichum acutatum

Sunken dark lesions on fruit, crown rot, runner blight. Spreads rapidly in warm, wet conditions.

VISK Solution: 50 ppm foliar spray. 100 ppm drench for crown treatment at planting.

Powdery Mildew

Podosphaera aphanis

White powdery coating on leaves, curling leaf edges, reduced fruit quality. Thrives in dry days with cool humid nights.

VISK Solution: 50 ppm foliar spray targeting leaf undersides. Begin preventive sprays when conditions favor mildew.

Phytophthora Crown Rot

Phytophthora cactorum

Sudden wilting and collapse of plants. Brown internal crown tissue. Often introduced through contaminated transplants.

VISK Solution: 100 ppm root dip before planting. 50 ppm soil drench monthly in wet conditions.

Application Protocol

Stage-by-Stage VISK Protocol for Strawberry

1

Runner / Transplant Prep

When: Before planting

  • Dip runners and transplants in 50 ppm VISK for 15 minutes. Sanitize planting beds with 200 ppm.
  • Key Risk: Crown rot (Phytophthora), transplant-borne pathogens

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Root dip

2

Establishment

When: Week 1–3

  • 50 ppm soil drench around root zone weekly. Sanitize drip lines before connecting.
  • Key Risk: Crown rot establishment, soil-borne Fusarium

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Soil drench

3

Vegetative Growth

When: Week 3–8

  • 50 ppm foliar spray every 7 days. 2–5 ppm continuous via drip.
  • Key Risk: Powdery mildew, leaf spot

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Foliar spray

4

Flowering

When: Week 6–10

  • 50 ppm fine mist every 5 days. Critical Botrytis prevention window — flowers are the entry point.
  • Key Risk: Botrytis infection through petals, anthracnose

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Fine mist

5

Green Fruit

When: Week 8–12

  • 50 ppm foliar spray every 7 days. Ensure coverage on developing fruit.
  • Key Risk: Anthracnose lesions forming, grey mold latent infection

Dilution: 50 ppm · 10 mL/100L · Spray

6

Ripening

When: Week 10–14

  • 30 ppm light spray every 5 days. Reduce volume to avoid fruit wetting — target surrounding foliage.
  • Key Risk: Botrytis activation on ripe fruit, fruit rot

Dilution: 30 ppm · 6 mL/100L · Light spray

7

Harvest

When: Ongoing

  • Sanitize harvesting tools and containers with 200 ppm. Handle berries with clean gloves.
  • Key Risk: Cross-contamination during picking, bruise-related decay

Dilution: 200 ppm · 40 mL/100L · Tool soak/spray

8

Post-Harvest

When: Immediately after picking

  • Gentle dip in 30 ppm VISK for 1–2 minutes. Air dry on sanitized racks. No rinsing needed.
  • Key Risk: E. coli, Salmonella, shelf life reduction

Dilution: 30 ppm · 6 mL/100L · Gentle dip
